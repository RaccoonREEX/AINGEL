## Research follow-up

The LSEC record identifies CC BY 4.0; its public ethics document states verified, signed consent for two identifiable participants covers recording, annotation, publication, public redistribution, derivatives, and AI/ML. That is a source statement, not AINGEL's independent audit of confidential consent forms. CC BY itself does not remove privacy and personality rights; review the exact uses and participant protections before displaying identifiable videos or generating a person's likeness. Research PUCE, EPN and UPS projects as reference leads, not automatically licensed corpora. Invite Deaf-community review only with explicit authorization to contact people. Do not transfer LSA/ASL performance claims to LSEC.

For memory, test factual retrieval with synthetic events, including empty, stale and conflicting evidence. For hearing, use consenting speakers or synthetic scripted audio to measure transcript accuracy/latency; no deafness classifier. For color, evaluate deterministic region sampling under varied lighting. Prefer original phrase boards and user-configured schedules over medical datasets. Track attribution per actual artifact, not merely by dataset family.


## Acquired evaluation subset

`prepare_vizwiz_eval.py` downloaded the official annotation archive after verifying the task's CC BY 4.0 statement. It read the 4,319 validation annotations and kept 15 answerable plus 15 unanswerable examples in ignored local storage. No image zip was downloaded and nothing was sent to a cloud model. `evaluate_vizwiz.py` accepts complete, unique per-image boolean predictions and computes subset answerability accuracy; it does not impersonate official VQA/AP scoring. No predictions or accuracy are fabricated. Actual image screening, inference and community validation remain pending.

## LSEC: public metadata prepared, not a sign-language translator

[`prepare_lsec_metadata.py`](../backend/scripts/prepare_lsec_metadata.py) retrieves only the fixed, 83,608-byte `lsec_metadata_1.0.0.zip` from the official Zenodo record, validates the record's published MD5 and a pinned SHA-256, and keeps the ZIP in ignored `.local/datasets/`. The aggregate [LSEC manifest](LSEC_DATASET_MANIFEST.json) contains no participant identifiers. The verified archive contains 17 metadata files: documentation, licensing and public consent statements, schemas, a code file and checksums. It has no corpus video or images. We did not execute the code inside the downloaded archive. The sentence-level archive (2.8 GB) and lexical archive (12.9 GB) were not downloaded.

```powershell
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe backend\scripts\prepare_lsec_metadata.py
```

Citation: Rubio Proaño et al., *AI-Ready Ecuadorian Sign Language Corpus*, version 1.0.1, 2026, [DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21627229](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21627229), [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Attribute the actual material used, link the license and indicate changes. The archive's `ACCESS_AND_ETHICS.md` describes verified consent and confidential institutional custody; `LICENSE.md` states that privacy, publicity and other personality rights are separate from the copyright license.

A metadata archive is not a trained model or evidence that translations are correct. Do not concatenate individual dictionary entries and present them as free-form LSEC interpretation. A first constrained communication experience needs complete, licensed phrase clips evaluated with Deaf LSEC users, explicit labeling and a fallback when a phrase is not covered. Real-time speech-to-LSEC generation additionally needs evaluated language translation, full signing motion, facial and body expression, latency and community-led accuracy evaluation. No clip playback or automatic sign translation has been enabled by this preparation.

For nonclinical communication design, the [W3C cognitive accessibility guidance](https://www.w3.org/TR/coga-usable/) supports clear, individually adapted language and [NIA guidance on communicating with a person with Alzheimer's](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/alzheimers-changes-behavior-and-communication/communicating-someone-who-has-alzheimers) recommends listening, giving time to respond and respecting the person. Choose support preferences with the person; do not infer personality or capability from a diagnosis. [MedlinePlus health-topic XML](https://medlineplus.gov/xml.html) offers public-domain summaries in Spanish and English, but [other MedlinePlus content is copyrighted](https://medlineplus.gov/about/using/usingcontent/). Cite the source, date content and keep medical questions within appropriate professional review.
